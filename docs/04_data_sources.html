4  Data sources

The data required to estimate all parameters discussed in the previous sections remain scarce and have only recently begun to appear in databases, dedicated initiatives, and the academic literature. Before the PFN (Earth Action, 2023), which has been cited throughout the previous sections as a relevant data source, the PLP (Peano et al., 2020) was the first initiative to support the development of emission factors for activities prone to releasing plastic particles into natural environments. The PLP Sectorial Guidelines are available online, and the access link is provided in Table 4.1, together with other relevant sources of data and information for practitioners seeking to quantify plastic emission flows from activities within the scope of their studies.

The PFN (Earth Action, 2023) is currently working to publish validated emission factors, although not all sectors have been assessed. At the time of writing, only three of the seven proposed modules have been completed and provide a complete dataset: packaging covering macroplastics from waste mismanagement and littering; textiles, covering macroplastics from end-of-life mismanagement and microplastics from production and use-phase wastewater leakage; and tire abrasion, covering microplastics emissions. The sectors already covered and those planned by the PFN are presented in Table 4.2. It is foreseen that as PFN data is generated or updated, the tool associated with this guide will be expanded to include them.

National statistics

Data on plastic emissions to the environment are also sometimes compiled by national agencies, with varying levels of granularity, often using material flow analysis (MFA) approaches. For example, Statistics Canada published the Physical Flow Account for Plastic Material in 2025 (Statistics Canada, 2025), which reports plastic flows and stocks from 2012 to 2021. Quantitative data on plastic leaked permanently to the environment are provided alongside other indicators, including total discarded plastic in products, the net stock of plastic in products remaining in use at the end of each year, and plastic products produced for Canadian consumption. These amounts are also reported for a range of product categories, from construction materials and electrical and electronic equipment to packaging, which is further subdivided into bottles, film, non-bottle rigid packaging and other packaging products. This type of data is particularly relevant for estimating emission factors associated with end-of-life management in Canada. Similar national or country-specific data sources may also be available for other contexts. For example, country-specific assessments have been carried out for Switzerland, providing detailed estimates of plastic flows and emissions that can support emission factor development (Kawecki & Nowack, 2019; Jiang & Nowack, 2025).

For information on the impact assessment models, useful data sources include, but are not limited to, the academic literature on the topic, especially the work of the MarILCA team and collaborators. These articles can support understanding of the meaning, scope, development approaches, and values of the CFs developed to date. Many of them also include case studies with transparent documentation of the assumptions and values used throughout the assessment, making them useful sources for inventory-related data as well.

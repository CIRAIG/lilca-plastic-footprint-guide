2  When should plastic impacts be accounted for?

Accounting for the impact of plastic litter requires additional attention at both the LCI and LCIA stages. Although some LCIA methods, such as IMPACT World+ v2.1 and GLAM, now include CFs for physical effects on biota, the corresponding elementary flows for plastic litter emissions are generally not available or are not systematically populated in commonly used LCI databases. Consequently, even when practitioners use an LCIA method that includes these CFs, plastic emissions often need to be quantified separately before they can be characterized.

In practice, this means that foreground data may need to be complemented with specific estimates of plastic leakage, while background datasets may not systematically include plastic emissions. Depending on the case study, practitioners may therefore need to perform additional calculations to estimate inventory flows, apply the CFs, or both. This additional work can be valuable, but it may not be necessary or significant for all assessments.

Users should therefore consider the following screening questions before proceeding with a full assessment of contributions from plastic litter emissions. A single yes or no answer does not in itself justify an inclusion or exclusion of plastic emissions in a study. Instead, these questions are meant to stimulate the reasoning of the practitioner regarding the inclusion of plastic emissions.

Questions to guide the decision on including plastic emissions in impact assessment studies.

2.1 Client and commissioner intent

  1. Has the client explicitly required the assessment of the environmental consequences of plastic litter within the study scope?
  1. Is the client particularly concerned with plastic litter as an environmental issue, whether for reputational, regulatory, or advocacy reasons?
  1. Are the study results intended for public disclosure or to support a comparative assertion, where omitting plastic litter impacts could be challenged or scrutinized?
  1. Is the study conducted within a regulatory or sectoral framework that mandates the reporting of plastic litter impacts?
  1. In your professional judgment, would omitting plastic litter impacts from this study scope constitute a material failure to adequately address the stated goal and intended application of the study, in a way that could not be justified on scientific or methodological grounds?

2.2 Goal and scope relevance

  1. Does the product system involve plastic materials that could plausibly be released as litter into the environment, as opposed to being used exclusively in industrial, closed-loop, or fully controlled-waste contexts?
  1. Is this a comparative study in which one or more alternatives are predominantly made of plastic while others are not, such that plastic litter impacts could be a differentiating factor between systems?

2.3 Materiality

  1. Does the product system contain a significant mass fraction of plastics relative to other materials?
  1. Do the plastics involved belong to categories with inherently high litter probability, such as single-use items, flexible food packaging, agricultural films, or fishing gear?
  1. Has a preliminary screening or hotspot analysis indicated that plastic litter flows are non-negligible relative to other inventory flows or impact categories in the system?

2.4 Foreground actionability

  1. Are the plastic litter flows that are likely to be significant located in the foreground system, where the practitioner can realistically model the emissions?

2.5 Feasibility

  1. Is reliable data available, whether primary or from well-documented secondary sources, to quantify plastic litter emission factors for the specific product categories, use contexts, and geographies covered by the study? This includes the sectors covered in this guide.

LiLCA (Litter Impacts in LCA) Plastic Footprint Guide

Preface

Life cycle assessment (LCA) is a standardized tool (ISO 14040) used to evaluate the impacts of products or services over their life cycle, from production to end-of-life, by evaluating impacts on ecosystem quality, human health, and ecosystem services and resources.

Up until recently, LCA of plastic products (and others) did not consider the impacts of plastic pollution emitted during their life cycles. New life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) metrics have allowed plastic pollution to be included in LCA, marking a significant step that should be adopted, especially for specific sectors such as packaging or textiles. As these advances are not generally known to the broader LCA community, this guide aims to facilitate integration of these new approaches in LCA.

Despite its title, this guide is not limited to conventional plastics. It covers:

  • conventional polymers (e.g., polyethylene, polypropylene, PET, PVC, polystyrene)
  • biodegradable and bio-based polymers (e.g., PLA, PHA, PBAT, polycaprolactone, starch blends)
  • natural cellulosic fibres (cotton, linen)
  • man-made cellulosic fibres (viscose/rayon, modal, lyocell)
  • tyre and road wear particles

Objective

The objective of this methodological guide is to provide LCA practitioners with a detailed understanding of how to assess potential impacts associated with plastic emissions. The guide provides step-by-step support for practitioners seeking to implement the relevant characterization factors (CFs) in their assessments, from the identification and preparation of relevant inventory flows to the calculation and interpretation of midpoint and damage results. At the time of writing the first edition of this guide, the available CFs covered in this guide are the ones assessing physical effects on biota from microplastic ingestion. Other CFs will be included in future revisions.

This guide includes steps to assess the amount of plastic emitted over the life cycle of products, and employs the CFs developed by MarILCA, which link the emissions of plastic to their potential impacts on ecosystem quality. These CFs were developed within the MarILCA framework. The MarILCA working group was created in 2018 with the support of the Life Cycle Initiative of UN Environment and the Forum for Sustainability through Life Cycle Innovation (FSLCI), with the objective of integrating potential environmental impacts of marine litter, especially plastic, into LCA results. This effort aims to provide a more comprehensive picture of potential environmental impacts and to support the identification of trade-offs associated with the use of plastic and other materials in product systems.

Because these CFs may not yet be readily available or fully operationalized in commonly used LCA software and LCI databases, practitioners may need to perform complementary calculations outside their usual modeling workflow. Depending on the software, database, and modeling approach used, this may concern the preparation of the LCI, the application of CFs during LCIA, or both.

Using this guide should therefore enable practitioners with different levels of experience to consistently integrate contributions from plastic emissions into their LCAs, either at midpoint or damage level, while making transparent the assumptions, data choices, and calculation steps required for implementation.

This guide follows the plastic footprint definition of Corella-Puertas et al. (2026). The plastic footprint is defined as (1) the mass of plastic litter entering the environment (with information on the polymer type, size, shape), and (2) its environmental impacts (quantified via impact assessment metrics). Here, the assessment focuses on the “plastic physical footprint”. A free online tool is available to facilitate the calculations outlined in this guide.

Content

This guide comprises six main sections following this preface.

  • Section 1 presents the rationale underpinning the development of the CFs, retraces their methodological development within the MarILCA framework, and explains what aspects of environmental impact they are intended to characterize. It situates the work within its broader methodological context and outlines both achieved milestones and ongoing developments.

  • Section 2 provides guidance on when the impact of plastic pollution should be accounted for in LCA studies. It discusses the practical implications of the current disconnect between available CFs and the limited representation of plastic litter flows in LCI databases. It then introduces screening questions to help practitioners determine whether further assessment is relevant and feasible.

  • Section 3 provides detailed step-by-step guidance for integrating the CFs into LCAs, covering the LCI, LCIA, and interpretation phases. Particular attention is given to key practical challenges, including the identification and selection of relevant inventory flows, the consistent application of CFs during impact assessment, and the robust interpretation of results.

  • Section 4 presents the main data sources currently available to support the quantification of plastic emissions in LCA studies. It reviews recent initiatives, including the Plastic Leak Project and the Plastic Footprint Network, that have contributed to the development of emission factors for activities and sectors prone to plastic leakage. The section also summarizes the current coverage of available data, highlights remaining gaps across sectors and emission pathways, and directs practitioners to relevant sources of information for estimating plastic emission flows within the scope of their assessments.

  • Section 5 presents fully developed case studies from selected industrial sectors, illustrating how the methodology is applied step by step and supporting correct implementation of the CFs. It also lists the published studies that include plastic impacts (Section 5.3).

  • Finally, Section 6 addresses recurring questions, frequently encountered issues, and methodological uncertainties. Users are encouraged to consult this section when encountering practical or conceptual challenges during their assessments.
